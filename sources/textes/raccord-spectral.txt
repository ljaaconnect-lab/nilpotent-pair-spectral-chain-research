Abstract
Let A = (aij) ∈Qn×n. I attach to every cell (i, j) the binary address wij = 2(i−1)n+j−1
and form the marked matrix BA(z)ij = pijzwij with pij = 1 + a2

and degree 2Θ(n2), yet it is computed exactly by an arithmetic circuit with fewer than 3n4
gates (n ≥2), without ever listing its monomials. A rectangular extension is obtained
by a Vandermonde complement. If every column carries an integer dj, every surviving

j dj; a number-theoretic transform

single universal sign (−1)n(n−1)/2 by doubling the orientation: each column carries a pair
qj = xjyj of exterior generators. The resulting product is computed by an explicit circuit

scalar compilation I give has exponential size. One step of the resulting pipeline is not
proved here: the correction of the signs carried by the coefficients of DA. I formulate
it as a precise conjecture about the signed polynomial DA alone, prove that every other
stage is polynomial, and prove that, conditionally on the conjecture, the whole pipeline
is polynomial. Finally, I give an exact scalar encoding of the corrected polynomial as the
central coefficient, at index nn −1, of an explicit polynomial in two variables computed by
a circuit of O(n4) gates. This reduces the conjecture, exactly, to the computation of that
central coefficient, which is the remaining step.

Introduction
The determinant of an n × n matrix is a signed sum over the n! permutations, and it is
evaluated in polynomial time by elimination or by the characteristic-polynomial recurrences of
Faddeev–LeVerrier, Csanky and Berkowitz [3, 5, 6, 4]. In that classical picture the individual
terms of the sum are never visible: only their sum is. The purpose of this paper is to make the
terms individually addressable while keeping the polynomial-time evaluation. To every cell (i, j)
of the matrix I attach a power zwij of a formal variable z, where the exponent wij = 2(i−1)n+j−1
is a binary address of the cell, and I study the polynomial

Overview of the argument
The proofs are organized in nine steps. Step 1 is combinatorial, Steps 2 to 5 are algebraic,
Steps 6 and 7 concern the spectral layer, and Steps 8 and 9 concern the sign correction and the
conjecture.
Step 1: binary addresses (Section 4). The bit positions 0, 1, . . . , n2 −1 are cut into n
consecutive blocks I1, . . . , In of length n, and the cell (i, j) is given the j-th bit of block Ii. For
an assignment s : [n] →[n] the exponent E(s) has exactly one bit in each block, so that s can be
read off from the binary expansion of E(s). This gives the injectivity E(s) = E(t) ⇒s = t, the
characterization of the set of addresses, and the comparison rule: E is ordered lexicographically
by (sn, . . . , s1). The extremal permutations are the identity and the reversal.

Conventions and notation
 N = {0, 1, 2, . . . } and [m] = {1, . . . , m}. An assignment is a map s : [n] →[n]; it is a
collision assignment if it is not injective. The set of assignments has nn elements and Sn
is the set of bijective ones.
 For σ ∈Sn, inv(σ) = #{(a, b) : a < b, σ(a) > σ(b)} and sgn(σ) = (−1)inv(σ). I use
the standard fact that if σ(a) > σ(a + 1) for some a, then τ = σ ◦(a a+1) satisfies
inv(τ) = inv(σ) −1, and that σ ̸= id has such an index a.
 A = (aij) ∈Qn×n, L is the total binary length of the numerators and denominators of the
aij, pij = 1+a2
ij and wij = 2(i−1)n+j−1. If aij = u/v in lowest terms then pij = (u2+v2)/v2,
whose numerator and denominator have together at most 2(len(u) + len(v)) + 2 bits; hence
the total length of the pij is at most 2L + O(n2).
 For a polynomial f ∈K[z], supp f is the set of exponents of its nonzero monomials, degz f
its degree, ordz f its valuation and [ze]f the coefficient of ze.
 An arithmetic circuit over a field K is a finite directed acyclic graph whose sources (leaves)
are labelled by the variable z or by elements of K, and whose other nodes (gates) have
fan-in two and are labelled + or ×. Each node computes a polynomial of K[z] in the
obvious way. The size is the number of gates. A multiplication by a constant, a subtraction
and a negation each cost one gate. A gate is a pointer to two earlier nodes; it does not
store the monomial expansion of the polynomial it computes (see [7, 8] for the standard
framework).
 All rings are commutative with unit, except the exterior algebras of Section 5, which are
graded-commutative.

Scope, limits and what is not claimed
(1) Universality of the rational filter. For pij = 1 + a2
ij, Theorem 1.1 holds for every finitely
encoded A ∈Qn×n, dense or sparse, structured or not. The value pij ≥1 is used only to
exclude vanishing coefficients; Remark 5.6 explains how to keep the amplitudes aij.
(2) Circuit versus expansion. A circuit of O(n4) gates is not a list of n! monomials; the latter
needs at least n!(n2−n+1) bits for the addresses alone (Proposition 6.2). The compactness
of Theorem 1.1 (v) is a statement about circuits, not about any expanded form.
(3) Numerical evaluation.
Specializing z to a number can destroy the separation of the
addresses; for instance z = 1 sends DA to det(pij), and the individuality of the monomials
is lost. The variable z must stay formal, or be evaluated in a setting where individual
monomials are no longer required.
(4) Reductions modulo a prime. Over FQ the support of ¯DA may shrink if some ¯pij = 0
(Remark 5.6); Theorem 1.2 holds for ¯DA regardless.
(5) The spectral layer.
Theorem 1.2 is exact in the exterior algebra.
Its circuit version
(Theorem 1.3) is polynomial in n and N; the naive layer-by-layer evaluation is not (Propo-
sition 11.1). The size bound is polynomial in n only if N is polynomial in n.
(6) Status of the sign correction. Conjecture 1.5 is not proved. Nothing stated as a theorem
in this paper depends on it except Theorem 1.6, which is explicitly conditional, and no
polynomial-size circuit for the corrected polynomial PA is claimed unconditionally. The
double orientation of Section 13 gathers all the signs into the single constant γn, but it does
not give a compact circuit for the resulting top coefficient: it reformulates the conjecture
rather than resolving it. The gate count 4n2 + n −2 of the doubled circuit is a count of
