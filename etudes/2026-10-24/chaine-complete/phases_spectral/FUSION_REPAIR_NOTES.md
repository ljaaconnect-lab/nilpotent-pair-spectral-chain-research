# Two precise attempts beyond a bounded terminal phase reader

These follow the exact small-case constructions in PHASE_CHAIN_NOTES.md.
They explore changes to the representation before terminal observation and
unbounded Fourier weights. Neither yields a general polynomial scalar DAG.
No frozen candidate, DAG implementation, development evaluator, or held-out
protocol was changed or run.

## 1. An exact differential fusion identity

Introduce auxiliary commuting variables X_ij and their commuting derivatives.
There is an exact identity over every commutative Q-algebra:

    det(partial/partial X_ij) det(M_ij X_ij) = per(M).

Indeed, a derivative permutation can survive on a determinant monomial only
when it selects exactly the same cells. The two permutation signs multiply
to +1; all differentiation factorials are 1. Put
M_ij=B_ij e_(d_j) in Q[Z/N] from the start. The result is then the complete
spectral output vector, with no need to construct its corrected scalar
amplitude before the spectral register. The auxiliary X variables are a
description of an operator action; they are not new allowed input leaves of
a finished scalar circuit.

The determinant of derivatives has a polynomial DAG if composition of
operators is treated as a primitive. A scalar implementation must compile
its action on the second determinant. This is the missing step; merely
applying the ordinary determinant gate bound to operator composition would
leave that action uncharged.

### 1.1 Attempt to use weighted Capelli polarizations

The Capelli identities relate an ordered determinant of polarizations to a
product of an ordinary determinant and a differential determinant. Their
noncommutative extensions require specified factorization conditions on
the cross-commutators; the oscillator representation is an auxiliary
algebra, not by itself a scalar-action algorithm. See Caracciolo, Sokal,
Sportiello [Part I](https://arxiv.org/abs/0809.3516), and Caracciolo,
Sportiello [Part II, Sections 1.1-1.2](https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/39288).

For the concrete weighted attempt define

    E_ij = sum_k X_ik B_jk partial_jk.

These are degree-preserving operators. Directly commuting the multiplication
and differentiation gives

    [E_ij,E_lm]
      = delta_jl sum_k X_ik B_jk B_mk partial_mk
        - delta_mi sum_k X_lk B_jk B_mk partial_jk.

The extra pointwise products of rows of B prevent closure on the n^2 E_ij
with the ordinary gl_n constants. However, a genuine polynomial Lie
closure exists: use A_ij^(k)=X_ik partial_jk, for n^3 generators. Then

    [A_ij^(k), A_lm^(s)]
      = delta_ks (delta_jl A_im^(k) - delta_mi A_lj^(k)).

Thus this attempt is not rejected on the false premise that its Lie
generator set is necessarily exponential.

### 1.2 The scalar diagonal-shift repair fails already at size two

For n=2, try the weighted analogue

    (E11+q) E22 - E21 E12  ?=  det(X) det(B_ij partial_ij).

Normal ordering gives the exact defect

    sum_(k=1)^2 X_2k B_2k (q-B_1k) partial_2k.

For nonzero generic B_21,B_22, it vanishes as an operator only if
q=B_11=B_12. For B=[[2,3],[5,7]], choosing q=2 leaves defect -7 on X11 X22;
choosing q=3 leaves defect +5 on X21 X12.

Allowing a whole scalar matrix Q does not fix this identity. In
col-det(E+Q), the additional first-order terms are

    q11 E22 + q22 E11 - q21 E12 - q12 E21.

Matching the independent X_1k partial_1k, X_1k partial_2k, and
X_2k partial_1k coefficients forces q22=q21=q12=0. The remaining equations
again force q11=B11=B12. This rules out this scalar-shift repair, even if
the q's are rational functions of B; it does not rule out operator-valued
shifts or other Capelli-type identities.

The same issue appears in the simplest factorized commutator hypothesis.
For multiplication by B_ij X_ij and transposed differentiation, the
cross-commutator tensor is proportional to B_ij delta_il delta_kj. A single
factorization U_il V_kj for a generic nonzero B forces U,V diagonal and
B_ij=U_ii V_jj. Thus the one-factor version fits separable row/column weights;
a generic B needs a sum of rank(B) such factors. No scalar action rule for
that general sum is obtained here.

### 1.3 Counting the action space, separately from the Lie generators

Consider column-multilinear X polynomials, with basis

    product_(k=1)^n X_(r_k,k),  r in [n]^n.

This space has dimension n^n. The unital algebra generated by the n^3
A_ij^(k) has a cyclic orbit spanning this entire space from det(X):

1. Apply product_k A_kk^(k) to select the diagonal monomial of det(X).
2. Apply product_k A_(r_k,k)^(k) to convert it into any specified basis
   monomial. The resulting coefficient is exactly 1.

This is a constructive independent family, with words of length at most
2n. Hence materializing that closed action module is exponential, despite
the polynomial number of Lie generators. It is not a lower bound on a
special-purpose circuit for the particular final contraction. Even certain
simple rank-one input weights can generate a large module while their final
permanent has a short formula, so the distinction is material.

Another literal action representation is the full family of derivatives of
det(B_ij X_ij), shared by their remaining row/column minors. It has
sum_k binom(n,k)^2=binom(2n,n) minor indices. If the contraction is ordered
by successive rows, only the remaining column subset need be stored,
giving the familiar 2^n-state alternative. These are explicit costs of two
representations, not unavoidable costs of every possible action compiler.

`verify_differential_repair.py` checks the differential identity formally in
every B monomial through n=5, verifies the weighted commutator on every
column-multilinear basis state through n=4 (67,787 operator checks), and
constructs all n^n independent orbit monomials through n=5. It also verifies
the size-two defect and records exact minor-state counts through n=12.

## 2. Unbounded Fourier weights are a distinct possibility

Suppose one seeks

    per(B) = sum_(t=1)^L c_t det(S_t elementwise-multiplied-by B),

where all entries of S_t have modulus 1. Setting B_ij=1 gives only

    (n!)^2 <= n^n L sum_t |c_t|^2.

There is no upper bound on the right-hand coefficient norm unless one is
proved. Coefficients with O(n log n) bits can already have the magnitudes
required by this inequality. Thus the bounded-reader Fourier-support bound
must not be applied to this enlarged model.

For the real phase subclass S_t in {+1,-1}^{n x n}, polynomial literal size
is especially not an obstruction. Remove dependent term vectors on S_n.
For the remaining L independent vectors choose L permutation rows on which
they form an invertible +/-1 matrix A. The coefficient equation is A c=b,
where b is a +/-1 parity vector. Cramer's rule and Hadamard imply that the
numerators and denominators of c have O(L log L) bits. This does not give
a polynomial-time way to find or certify the phase matrices, but it shows
that a polynomial-size representation could have legal rational literals.

### 2.1 A complete four-term coefficient reduction

For L<=4, all possible nonzero absolute coefficient patterns in an
independent representation can be enumerated by exact rational arithmetic.
Multiply rows by b to make the target all +1; multiply columns by the first
row signs to make that row all +1. The latter changes only coefficient signs.
There are 2^(L(L-1)) normalized square +/-1 matrices to inspect.

The resulting possible absolute patterns are:

| Independent terms | Patterns |
|---:|---|
| 1 | (1) |
| 2 | none |
| 3 | (1,1,1) |
| 4 | (1/2,1/2,1/2,1/2) or (1,1,1,2) |

`probe_unbounded_phases.py` performs this exact enumeration. No bound on
unused-score values is imposed. At n=5 the four-half pattern is impossible
by the norm inequality: L sum |c_t|^2=4, while (5!)^2/5^5=576/125>4.

The other pattern leads to a concrete cell-phase constraint problem. Give
each of four terms arbitrary cell bits after first-row/column gauge and
an arbitrary global sign. If b_t(sigma) is the resulting parity bit, the
identity with absolute coefficients (1,1,1,2) is exactly

    b1+b2+b3+2 b4 = 2 for even sigma,
    b1+b2+b3+2 b4 = 3 for odd sigma.

All 120 permutations at n=5 are included. CP-SAT returns INFEASIBLE; the
recorded run took 0.64 seconds, 24,640 branches and 5,389 conflicts. A
three-term (1,1,1) representation would be covered by splitting one signed
term into -1 and +2 copies, while a one-term representation embeds using
1+1+1-2. Thus this also covers dependent representations with fewer terms.
This is reproducible finite solver evidence against <=4 real signed-cell
determinants at n=5, combined with an exact coefficient reduction. It is
not a solver-independent general lower bound or a result about complex
phases.

The number of determinants may grow polynomially; this experiment does not
exclude that case. Its point is to test a genuine unbounded-weight repair
with all cell bits free, not to reapply the bounded Fourier theorem under
a missing hypothesis. No larger family or uniform compiler emerges here.

## 3. Outcome

The differential identity changes the action before terminal observation
and the weighted determinant mixture changes the reader's global bounds.
Both are precise extensions of the original hypothesis. The first has an
exact polynomial Lie-generator closure but no polynomial scalar action
found, and its simplest Capelli shift fails explicitly. The second survives
the general norm obstruction when weights grow, but the fully tested
four-term real-phase instance fails at n=5. Neither is a complete solution
of the general joint compiler problem, and neither is used to reject it.
